Broadsheet Track

An exploration of the letterpress direction, not a replacement for the system you accepted. Two surfaces, because this aesthetic behaves completely differently on each one.

Read this first. The poster below can go all the way, because the landing page is already planned as a Long Document with a newspaper masthead — it has scale and emptiness to spend. The book page cannot: heavy condensed display, offset colour layers and two saturated hues all need room, and at 390 px with seven characters there is none. So the app takes the vocabulary — mono caps, rule pairs, cream stock, one brick red — and leaves the scale on the poster. Judge the two separately.

A reading companion for books put down and picked up again

Put it down. Pick it up.

Three weeks pass. You open the book and you have no idea who she is, or why he owes her anything. That is not a comprehension problem — you understood it fine at the time. It is a recall problem, and it only shows up on the way back in.

So write the cast down as you go. Names, a line each, and who is what to whom. Then the way back in is a glance.

What carried across, and what stayed on the poster

Carried · mono caps

Every label is now IBM Plex Mono, uppercase, letterspaced 0.14–0.16em. This is the single loudest signal of the reference and it costs nothing at density — labels are short by nature. Relationship types are the biggest win: they read as a printed index.

Carried · cream stock

Paper moved from oklch(98.5%) to oklch(93% 0.014 84) — a real cream rather than a barely-warm white. Every neutral moved with it, so the hairlines had to darken to L 74% to stay visible on the darker ground.

Carried · brick red + rule pairs

Accent swaps spruce for oklch(43% 0.15 30), 4.8:1 on cream. Section heads gain a 2 px rule and the masthead a 3 px + 1 px pair. Buttons lose their radius entirely — square corners are letterpress, rounded ones are software.

Stayed · Anton and the offset layer

The poster face appears on the landing hero and nowhere else. A condensed 7 rem display cannot live in a list, and the offset ghost layer needs the letters to be enormous or it reads as a rendering fault. Anton is not loaded by the app screens at all.

Stayed · the second colour

The pale teal is a poster device, one element, one place. Two saturated hues in a dense list would break both the accent budget and your “no intense colours” brief. It exists as --ghost and the app never reads it.

Dashboard · one route, two states

/ is the only unguarded page in the product — middleware.ts protects /books and /dashboard. Signed out it is the poster; signed in it is A2. The masthead loses Sign in and gains Sign out; a signed-in reader never sees a sign-in control anywhere.

Dashboard · why Stat-Led

An Anton numeral over a mono caption is how a broadsheet prints a figure, so the stats need no new vocabulary. It is also a different macrostructure from the poster, so the two states of one route do not read as the same page with the words swapped.

Dashboard · three integers, and that is the ceiling

idea-notes.md rules out time tracking and reading progress; § Non-Goals rules out counters, percentages and streaks. Collection size is not progress — but this screen is exactly where that line gets crossed. No percentages, no pages, no charts, no “on a roll”. Relationship count is not a fourth stat: nobody asks that question.

Dashboard · the zero state is required

A new reader would otherwise meet three giant zeros on their first screen, which reads as failure. Below one book the stats do not render at all — A3 replaces them. A zero character count with a book present is fine and shows as a real zero.

Dashboard · known redundancy

At full width the rail already lists the library with counts, so Books and Finished restate what is two inches to the left. It earns its place on three grounds: Characters appears nowhere else, there is no rail below 60 rem, and the bare domain has to lead somewhere. If it still feels redundant after real use, drop the counts on desktop rather than defend them.

The honest cost

Cream at L 93% is 5.5 points darker than the accepted paper — closer to “light” than to “bright”. Body serif at 0.9 rem in a dense list is more work to read than a UI sans. Both are defensible; neither is free. Compare against the other mockup on the same phone before choosing.
